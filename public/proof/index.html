The Paid Layer

Ads showed up in month 8. They only buy what organic hasn't taken yet.

Here's the honest version. For seven months this site ran on organic search alone. In July I turned on a small Google Ads account for one reason: the biggest search in the market, "junk removal san diego" at 2,400 a month, still belongs to competitors with years of reviews behind them. Ads bridge that gap while the site earns it. Every campaign targets a search where the site is invisible or still climbing. The searches it already owned were never bought.

Organic still does the heavy lifting, at $0 a click.

The Leak

The rankings worked. Then I found where the money was leaking.

Tracking every lead against booked revenue shows something rankings never will: speed decides who gets the job. On this site, leads answered in under 20 minutes closed at meaningfully higher rates than leads that sat for hours. And some leads never got answered at all.

That's not the owner's fault. He's on a job. His hands are full. The phone rings, the caller hits voicemail, hangs up, and calls the next company on Google. The site did its job. The job still went to someone else.

That isn't a marketing problem. The marketing worked. It's a capture problem. So I built the fix.

The Fix

Your voicemail loses jobs. Mine asks questions, texts them back, and books them.

And every Monday you get a plain email: how many calls you missed, and how many turned into jobs.

When you can't pick up, it answers instead of a dead-end voicemail.
